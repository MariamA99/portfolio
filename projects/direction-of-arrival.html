Direction-of-Arrival Estimation

Direction-of-Arrival Estimation Neural Networks Frequency-Diverse Antennas Terahertz Communications

DoA estimation using frequency diverse antenna.

Abstract

Accurate direction-of-arrival (DoA) estimation is critical for reliable terahertz communications, where high propagation losses and line-of-sight requirements demand precise alignment between transceivers. This research investigates frequency-diverse antennas combined with machine learning to estimate signal arrival directions within the IEEE 802.15.3d band. The antennas produce direction-dependent spectral responses, which neural network models learn to associate with the corresponding angles of arrival. The first approach employs an intentionally asymmetric antenna and a convolutional neural network (CNN), achieving a reported validation mean squared error of 3.9° and root mean squared error of 1.9°, with performance assessed under different propagation obstacles and absorbers. The second approach develops a manufacturable all-dielectric frequency-diverse antenna and extends the method to both single- and multiple-source estimation. Experiments demonstrate DoA tracking at transmitter–receiver separations up to 900 mm, with average root mean squared errors of 2.53° for single sources and 4.56° for multiple sources. Together, these studies demonstrate compact, data-driven approaches to angular estimation in next-generation terahertz communication systems.
