Automatic Modulation Recognition

CNN BiLSTM Terahertz Communications

Abstract

Terahertz communications, enabled by photonics-based transceivers, offer ultra-wide bandwidths and support dynamic multi-rate transmission, making adaptive modulation and automatic modulation recognition (AMR) important capabilities for maintaining reliable link performance. However, despite extensive AMR research in microwave and millimetre-wave systems, its application to terahertz communications remains limited, particularly in experimental settings, where distinct propagation effects and hardware impairments make reliable modulation recognition significantly more challenging. In this work, we investigate deep learning-based AMR over experimental photonics-based terahertz links and assess the simulation-to-experimental generalization of classifiers trained on simulated signals. A convolutional neural network (CNN) and bidirectional long short-term memory (BiLSTM) model are trained on simulated complex-valued baseband signals and evaluated on experimental non-coherent and coherent terahertz links. The results show successful transfer from simulation to experiment, with the BiLSTM exhibiting greater robustness to phase offsets and IQ imbalance and reliable operation over a broader range of data rates than the CNN.

Publications and further reading

Part of this research has been published in the proceedings of the 2025 International Conference on Infrared, Millimeter, and Terahertz Waves (IRMMW-THz). An extended journal manuscript, incorporating further analysis and experimental validation, is currently under review.
